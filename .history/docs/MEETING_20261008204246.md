### Meetings Note from Amar and Jean

The provided **transcripts** document a series of student project meetings with the founders of **Stay in Canada**, an immigration consultancy business, to develop a specialized web application by the end of November. The **discussions** focus on understanding the daily **workflows**, operational challenges, and communication hurdles faced by Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultants (RCICs) and their administrative staff. A primary area of **difficulty** highlighted is the repetitive back-and-forth communication required when clients submit incomplete or mismatched information through traditional intake forms. To address these **inefficiencies**, the students propose creating a centralized **digital portal** featuring a user-friendly smart intake questionnaire, automated document validation via artificial intelligence, and integrated status tracking. While the company **leadership** acknowledges the potential value of streamlining the client intake and document verification process, they emphasize that final approval responsibilities must remain strictly with the certified consultants.

Summary

### **1\. Summary of BCIT 2.m4a (Project Proposal Meeting with Omar)**

- **Context**: The BCIT student developer and design team met with **Omar** (Business, Marketing, and Financial Partner at Stay in Canada) to present their proposed project scope focused on automating and streamlining the client intake and document verification process[1][2].
- **Proposed Workflow &amp; Features**:
  - **Lifecycle Alignment**: The proposed system starts **after** a lead signs a retainer agreement and pays their down payment/invoice[3].
  - **Smart Intake Questionnaire**: Replaces long, intimidating forms with a step-by-step digital questionnaire tailored for clients, particularly benefiting those with language barriers[3][6].
  - **AI Verification &amp; Cross-Checking**: AI checks for missing form fields, spelling errors, and cross-references data entered in the questionnaire against uploaded supporting documents (e.g., verifying passport spelling or date of birth against entered form fields)[3].
  - **Document Management**: Provides a document checklist, enforces document size limits (**4 MB maximum per file** as required by IRCC)[10], and gives staff a checklist to verify missing uploads[3].
  - **Client Portal &amp; Status Tracking**: Includes an application status tracker to give clients transparency[3][11], auto-saving form entries[12], and an in-portal communication channel to minimize email back-and-forth[12].
- **Omar's Key Feedback**:
  - Emphasized that **AI verification matching form inputs against uploaded documents** is the most valuable aspect of the proposal[7].
  - Asked if the system could automatically populate official **IRCC PDF forms** directly (sharing a link to a demo tool like Visa.ca)[14][15]. The dev team agreed to research backend feasibility[13][16], while noting potential technical, legal, and scope limitations[17].
- **Next Steps**: Agreed to conduct weekly progress update meetings on **Wednesdays after 11:30 AM**[20][21].

### **2\. Summary of BCIT 3.m4a (Operations &amp; RCIC Interview)**

- **Context**: The student team interviewed the **RCIC Partner** handling operations at Stay in Canada to understand their day-to-day workflow, toolchain, and major operational friction points[2].
- **Current Operational Workflow**:
  - **Role Division**: A small operational team where the RCIC partner manages operations while Omar handles business, finance, and marketing[2].
  - **Lead to Client Pipeline**: Inquiries are logged -&gt; directed to book consultations -&gt; retainer agreement signed via **Zoho CRM** \-&gt; invoice issued -&gt; welcome aboard email sent containing a **Zoho Drive** folder link with the **Client Information Form (CIF)**, Authorized Rep Form, and document list[24].
  - **Toolchain Transition**: Currently using **HubSpot CRM**, **Zoho CRM**, **Zoho Drive**, and **Zoho Click** for internal communication, with an ongoing effort to consolidate tools into the Zoho ecosystem[28].
  - **Turnaround Target**: Aim for a **2-week turnaround** from receiving the final required documents to submitting the application to IRCC (though complex cases like spousal sponsorships take longer)[32][33].
- **Primary Operational Pain Points**:
  - **Incomplete or Inaccurate CIFs**: Clients frequently omit information (family/sibling details, occupations), provide inconsistent dates compared to their resumes or prior IRCC applications, or upload untranslated documents[34].
  - **Repetitive Communication**: Going back and forth to fix minor errors causes frustration for both clients and staff and significantly delays submissions[34].
  - **Manual Entry Errors**: Admin staff manually create client accounts on government portals and manually key in information, creating room for human error[34][38].
- **Feedback on Student Project**:
  - Confirmed that a streamlined, user-friendly digital **Client Information Form (CIF)** that prevents missing or mismatched data would provide the highest immediate value to operations[39][40].
  - Noted that document requirement checklists are strictly provided **only after** a client has paid the initial retainer fee[41][42].
  - Agreed to arrange user research interviews with representative **B2C and B2B clients**[43][44].
